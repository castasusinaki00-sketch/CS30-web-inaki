Academic Analytical Essay • ENG120-0 A70

Human vs. AI: A Comparative Study of Writing Effectiveness

An extended causal analysis examining how generative AI functions as an academic survival strategy among Mapúa University students, its role in cognitive offloading, and the emergence of the performance confidence gap.

Author: Iñaki Carmelo Sanderz E. Castasus Faculty: Asst. Prof. Jane C. Mangalus, M.A. Affiliation: Mapúa University Department of Liberal Arts

Thesis Statement

Cognitive Offloading

While Generative AI provides Mapúa University students with an essential 'safety net' for managing heavy academic workloads, its long-term use leads to a form of cognitive offloading that creates a performance gap between AI-assisted output and independent writing proficiency.

Causal Analysis

Conceptual Research Framework

Mapping the cause-and-effect chain from environmental pressure to writing proficiency impact.

Input: Academic Environment

Triggers & Demands

  • Fast-paced Mapúa quarterly curriculum
  • Heavy technical academic workload
  • Writing anxiety and impending deadlines
Development: Process

Cognitive Offloading

  • AI-assisted brainstorming and ideation
  • Automating initial essay outlines and drafts
  • Polishing and refining syntax via LLMs
Finalization: Output

Writing Proficiency Impact

  • High-quality final essay output
  • Improved technical grammar and coherence
  • Psychological dependency on AI "safety net"

Investigation

Research Objectives

Three central questions exploring confidence, ideation, and skill atrophy.

01

Confidence Shift

Why do students feel more (or less) confident in their writing skills while using AI?

02

Ideation Quality

How does AI affect brainstorm ideas compared to traditional manual brainstorming?

03

Long-Term Autonomy

What is the effect of long-term AI use on a student's independent writing ability?

Descriptive Statistics

Key Findings & Survey Results

Quantitative survey data collected from 15 undergraduate students across engineering, computing, and foundational disciplines.

Table 1: Frequency of Use

  • Fact-checking citations 4.67 (Always)
  • Grammar correction 3.47 (Often)
  • Deadline brainstorming 3.13 (Neutral)
  • Initial outline generation 3.07 (Neutral)
  • Submitting >50% AI text 2.20 (Rarely)

Table 2: Perceived Proficiency

  • Helpful as concept tutor 4.67 (Always)
  • Independent essay ability 4.02 (Agree)
  • AI idea quality vs. manual 3.67 (Agree)
  • Academic stress reduction 3.08 (Neutral)
  • Research info accuracy 3.00 (Neutral)

Core Behavioral Findings

The Safety Net

Students use AI as a technical buffer against strict rubrics, causing initial writer's block when attempting to compose unassisted.

The Confidence Gap

While submitted papers receive high marks, respondents report doubt in their personal ability to replicate that standard independently.

Academic Endurance

Adoption is driven by survival in GED, CWTS, and technical courses rather than academic dishonesty.

Thematic Analysis

Student Voices & Lived Experience

Qualitative responses highlighting how students navigate ethics, workload, and authentic voice.

Academic Survival

"Students utilized AI as a survival necessity when faced with numerous, simultaneous deadlines, heavy workloads, or weekly long essays under intense mental exhaustion."

Authentic Voice & Ownership

"Students feel ownership only when the core ideas originate from them. Heavy AI revision is avoided because it removes the 'human essence of struggle, imperfection, and nuance.'"

Where the Line is Drawn

"Students consciously reject AI when it replaces independent thinking or understanding. Work is acceptable for outlining and syntax, but unacceptable for bypassing comprehension."

  • Brainstorming & Ideation
  • Overcoming Writer's Block
  • Fixing Sentence Structure
  • Concept Clarification
  • Citation Formatting

Scope & Constraints

Study Limitations

  • Sample size limited to 15 undergraduate Mapúa students
  • Reliance on self-reported survey responses and respondent bias
  • Conducted within a single academic quarter (cross-sectional)
  • Did not isolate differences between individual LLM models (e.g., ChatGPT-4 vs. Grammarly)

Future Directions

Recommendations

  • Expand sample size across multiple universities and faculties
  • Conduct multi-year longitudinal studies evaluating unassisted writing retention
  • Incorporate faculty assessment data to evaluate grading disparities
  • Analyze the psychological impact of AI in high-stakes thesis and capstone projects

Final Implication

Preserve the Human Element in Composition

Generative AI is an indispensable survival tool for modern academic rigor. However, preserving critical thinking requires treating AI as an interactive tutor rather than an intellectual proxy.
