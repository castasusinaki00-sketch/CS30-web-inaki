Human vs. AI: A Comparative Study of Writing Effectiveness among Mapúa University Students

Author: Iñaki Carmelo Sanderz E. Castasus

Summary

This study examines why Mapúa University students use Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) in essay writing and how this affects their independent writing abilities. The research argues that although AI provides valuable academic support and helps students cope with heavy workloads, long-term dependence may weaken writing proficiency through a process called cognitive offloading, where students allow AI to perform tasks that would normally develop their critical thinking and writing skills.

Background

Academic writing is a key requirement at Mapúa University. The rise of AI tools such as ChatGPT has transformed how students approach writing tasks by assisting with brainstorming, grammar correction, summarization, outlining, and even full essay generation.

Research Questions

  1. Why do students feel more or less confident in their writing when using AI?
  2. How does AI-assisted brainstorming compare with manual brainstorming?
  3. What are the long-term effects of AI use on independent writing ability?

Methodology

The study used a qualitative case study with a mixed-method approach. Fifteen Mapúa University students participated in surveys containing rating-scale questions and open-ended responses.

Key Findings

1. AI as a Writing Safety Net

  • Grammar correction: Often used (3.47)
  • Outline generation: Sometimes used (3.07)
  • Deadline-driven brainstorming: Sometimes used (3.13)
  • Mostly AI-generated essays: Rarely submitted (2.20)
  • Fact-checking AI outputs: Always performed (4.67)

2. AI vs. Manual Brainstorming

  • AI idea quality rated positively (3.67)
  • Reduces stress moderately (3.08)
  • Research accuracy rated neutral (3.00)
  • Students still believe they can write independently (4.02)
  • AI highly valued as a tutor (4.67)

3. Long-Term Effects

Safety Net and Dependency

Students increasingly rely on AI to begin assignments, creating dependency and reducing confidence when writing without assistance.

Confidence Gap

Students feel confident in AI-assisted outputs but less confident about their own writing skills.

Survival Strategy

AI is primarily used to cope with heavy workloads, deadlines, and academic stress rather than to avoid work.

Student Perspectives

  • Brainstorming and outlining
  • Drafting content
  • Grammar checking
  • Research assistance
  • Explaining difficult concepts

Conclusion

Generative AI improves the technical quality of student writing and provides valuable academic support. However, excessive reliance may reduce independent writing confidence, critical thinking, and authentic voice. The study recommends balancing AI assistance with independent practice to maintain writing proficiency.

Limitations

  • Small sample size (15 students)
  • Self-reported responses
  • Short study duration
  • Single-university focus

Recommendations

  • Use larger sample sizes
  • Conduct longitudinal studies
  • Include professor perspectives
  • Compare different AI functions and platforms
  • Study AI use in thesis and high-stakes writing
